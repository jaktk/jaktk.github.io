This thesis, conducted in support of Sustainable Cape Maclear's solid waste management strategy for Cape Maclear, Malawi, addressed the village's informal glass dumpsites through three linked subtasks: designing and building a glass crusher, proposing waste valorization pathways, and characterizing dumpsites. A manually operated roller-mill crusher was designed, but its drivetrain lacked sufficient structural strength to crush intact bottles; a simpler pre-crusher was therefore built to break bottles into shards before roller-mill processing, together producing particle sizes from powder to several centimeters within an acceptable processing time. Several glass valorization pathways were identified, differing in economic potential, particle-size suitability, and throughput capacity, for Sustainable Cape Maclear to select from. Dumpsite quantification and characterization were dropped due to time constraints, yielding only a map of dumpsite locations.
